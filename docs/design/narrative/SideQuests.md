# Side Quests

> **Purpose:** How quests grant abilities, the canonical example, and the fetch-quest-overload pitfall to design against.
> **Owner:** Franco Fusaro · **Status:** Living · **Last Updated:** 2026-07-10
> **Related:** [NPCs](NPCs.md), [../gameplay/Progression](../gameplay/Progression.md), [Bosses](Bosses.md)

## Quests grant abilities

Quests are one of the three ability sources (found / taught / earned — see
[gameplay/Progression](../gameplay/Progression.md)). A quest-giver NPC rewards an
ability for a task; the reward's flavor matches the deed and the cat that could help.

## Canonical example

A nervous bird, its eggs under threat, begs the fast, fearless **Orange** cat to defend
the nest / fetch food → Orange learns a combat/pounce ability. A slow elder cat who only
trusts the patient **Tuxedo** → teaches a Loaf/anchor upgrade.

## Pitfall to design against — fetch-quest overload

"Bring me food / protect my eggs" is a fetch/escort quest — fine in small doses, deadly
in bulk. Rules:
- Keep quests **short, diegetic**; favor **protect / escort / reach** over pure fetch.
- **Mix** quest-unlocks with world-found unlocks so it never becomes an errand simulator.
- **Never** gate a *core traversal* ability behind a tedious quest.
